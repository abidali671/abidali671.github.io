What to Put in a Freelance Invoice (and What to Check Before You Send It)

By Abid Ali, freelance full-stack developer · November 16, 2026

  • Freelancing
  • Invoices
  • Payments
  • Beginners

An invoice looks like a simple document until you send one that is missing a date, an invoice number or a description the client can recognise. Then the payment sits, not because the client does not want to pay, but because their accounts person cannot match the file to a job.

I'm a freelance full-stack developer in Karachi, and I treat the invoice as part of the work, not as an afterthought. This post is a practical list of what I put on a freelance invoice, what I check before I send it, and how that fits when the job ran through Upwork or Fiverr rather than a direct transfer.

The wider money side, especially if you work from Pakistan, is covered in freelancer tax paperwork in Pakistan and in how to reconcile Upwork earnings with a PKR payout. I will not repeat those tax or payout steps here. I will stay with the document itself.

Why the invoice still matters on a marketplace

On Upwork and Fiverr, a lot of the payment record lives on the platform. You still often need your own invoice: for a direct client, for a job that is paid outside a marketplace, for your own records, or for a client who has to show a document to their accounts team.

Even when the platform already has a receipt, a clear invoice you keep for yourself makes later paperwork easier. The invoice is the story of one job, on one page: who paid whom, for what, and when.

The fields I always include

These are the lines I will not send without.

Who it is from. Your name, as you use it on official documents, and a way to reach you. If you have a business name you invoice under, use that consistently. A city and country are enough of an address for many small jobs; if a client needs more, they will usually say so.

Who it is to. The client's name or company name, exactly as they have asked you to write it. If they sent a billing name that is different from the name in the chat, use the billing name.

An invoice number. A unique identifier you can find again. I use a simple pattern: the year, then a number I increment. You can also include a short client code. What matters is that two invoices never share a number.

The invoice date, and the period or delivery date the work belongs to. If the job ran for two weeks in March, say so. If it is a single delivery, the delivery date is enough.

A plain description of the work. Not "services." The name of the project and what was delivered: "Five-page clinic website, including contact form and mobile layout" is something an accounts person can understand. If there were milestones, list them.

The amount, the currency, and whether the figure is the one they should pay. If a platform fee comes out before the money reaches you, do not invent a rate in the invoice. State the agreed amount in the currency you agreed, and, if you need to show a fee, use the figure from your own account, not a number you copied from a blog post.

How to pay, and by when. For a marketplace job, "Paid through Upwork, contract [name or ID]" may be enough. For a direct job, name the method you have already agreed and the due date. Do not add a new payment method on the invoice if you have not discussed it.

The status, if it is useful. "Due," "Paid on [date]," or "Paid through [platform]" stops the client wondering whether they are looking at a request or a record.

Useful extras, when they apply

Not every invoice needs every extra line. Add these when they help.

  • A purchase order number, if the client gave you one.
  • A breakdown of milestones or of hourly time, if that is how you agreed to charge.
  • A short note on revisions that were included, if that was part of the price.
  • Your tax identifier, if you have one and the client or your own records need it. Digital Dialogue's guide to freelancer tax paperwork in Pakistan explains the paperwork side for freelancers in Pakistan in more depth than I will go into here.
  • A note of the platform contract or order ID, so the invoice and the marketplace record can be matched later, including when you later follow a guide such as how to reconcile Upwork earnings with a PKR payout.

I do not add decoration. A logo is fine if you have one. A long biography is not.

What I check before I send it

I read the invoice once as if I were the person who has to pay it, and once as if I were the person who has to find it in six months.

  1. Names match. The client's billing name is right, and my own name is the one I use for this work.
  2. The description matches the job. A client with two projects should be able to tell which one this is.
  3. The currency is the one you agreed. A number without a currency is a future argument.
  4. The amount matches the agreement. I compare it with the contract, the offer or the last written quote, not with my memory.
  5. The invoice number has not been used before. I keep a simple list.
  6. The payment instructions are complete, or the marketplace reference is complete.
  7. I am sending the final file, as a PDF, to the place we already use for the job, not a draft screenshot from a chat.

If anything in that list is wrong, I fix the file before I send it. A corrected invoice is normal. Two corrected invoices for the same job start to look careless.

Marketplace jobs and your own copy

When the job is on Upwork or Fiverr, I still keep my own copy with the same number and description. The platform record is the legal payment path for that job; my invoice is the record I can find without logging into the platform. If the client asks for a PDF on their letterhead style, I can send it without inventing a second set of figures.

If a client asks me to invoice a different amount from the one on the platform, I slow down. That can be a simple request for a document that shows a local figure, or it can be a request I should not meet. I do not invent numbers, and I do not write a document that contradicts the official payment.

A simple habit that keeps this easy

I start the invoice when the job starts, not on the day I want to be paid. I fill in the names, the description and the number while the agreement is still in front of me. At the end I only add the date, the final amount and the status.

That habit has saved me from sending a vague invoice at the end of a tiring week. The freelance invoice generator is a convenient way to get a clean PDF if you do not already have a template. Use whatever tool you like, as long as the fields above are present and the file is one you could explain later.

An invoice is a short document, and it is also a piece of professional writing. If the client can understand it, their accounts person can process it, and you can find it again, it has done its job.
